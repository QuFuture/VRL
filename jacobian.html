Jacobian and Singularities

The Jacobian J(q) maps joint rates to tool velocity: v = J q̇. On a two-link planar arm (the first two links of a SCARA) you can see this map as an ellipse at the tool, and watch it flatten into a line when the arm reaches a singular pose.

Manipulability across θ₂

w = |l₁l₂ sin θ₂| does not depend on θ₁. It is largest with the elbow at ±90° and zero when the arm is fully stretched or folded.

Theory

The tool position of the 2R arm is

x = l₁cos θ₁ + l₂cos(θ₁+θ₂) y = l₁sin θ₁ + l₂sin(θ₁+θ₂)

Differentiating with respect to time gives v = J q̇ with

J = ⎡ −l₁s₁ − l₂s₁₂ −l₂s₁₂ ⎤ ⎣ l₁c₁ + l₂c₁₂ l₂c₁₂ ⎦ det J = l₁ l₂ sin θ₂

When det J = 0 the arm is singular: the tool cannot move in one direction however fast the joints turn, and J⁻¹ does not exist.

Procedure

  1. With θ₂ = 90°, set θ̇₁ = 30°/s and θ̇₂ = 0. Check that v is perpendicular to the line from the base to the tool.
  2. Change θ₁ only. Confirm det J and the ellipse shape do not change, only its orientation.
  3. Move θ₂ towards 0°. Watch σ₂ shrink and κ grow until the ellipse becomes a line.
  4. Switch to inverse mode and ask for ẋ = 0.2 m/s. Press Run and record the joint rates as the arm nears full stretch.
  5. Plot your recorded θ̇ against det J. Explain why joint rates blow up near a singularity.
  6. Set l₁ = l₂ and find where the inner workspace boundary disappears.

What the ellipse means

  • All joint-rate vectors with ‖q̇‖ = 1 rad/s map to tool velocities on this ellipse.
  • Its semi-axes are the singular values σ₁, σ₂ of J, along the eigenvectors of J Jᵀ.
  • Manipulability w = σ₁σ₂ = |det J| is proportional to the ellipse area.
  • κ = σ₁/σ₂ measures how uneven the arm's dexterity is. κ = 1 is isotropic, κ → ∞ is singular.
